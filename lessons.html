Lessons Learned

Reflections on our project journey and key takeaways for future success

Partnership & Initial Contact

February 5, 2026

What Went Well

  • Partnership with SHP–NRV confirmed quickly through a brief phone call with Paul Mele
  • Full team attendance (Holden, Asher Tillman, Luis, Andres, Weijin Hsu) showed strong commitment
  • Paul was supportive and open to collaborating with the student team
  • Confirmed that existing SHP processes are acceptable for the course, reducing the need to create a new project from scratch

Challenges Encountered

  • The initial call was very short (10 minutes), leaving many operational details unaddressed
  • No specific project activities or roles were defined during this first contact
  • The team had limited knowledge of SHP’s build process and operational needs at this stage

What We Learned

  • A quick introductory call is effective for confirming partnership interest, but a longer in-person meeting is needed for detailed planning
  • The stakeholder (Paul Mele) values a structured project management approach with defined roles, timelines, and milestones
  • April 25 was identified early as the project end date, anchoring all future planning

PM Takeaway

Stakeholder Engagement: Early and direct contact with the primary stakeholder set a positive tone for the project. Understanding Paul’s expectation for structured project management helped align the team’s approach from the very beginning.

Kickoff Meeting & Project Scoping

February 9, 2026

What Went Well

  • In-depth face-to-face meeting at Panera Bread covered all key operational areas (funding, builds, delivery process)
  • Paul provided detailed insight into SHP’s funding model (local donors, churches, national distribution across 350 chapters)
  • Gained clear understanding of the bed build process (components, staining, mattress, bedding)
  • Two key dates established: April 10 (observation build) and April 25 (main build targeting 40 beds)
  • All team members present, no absences

Challenges Encountered

  • Several action items left with “TBD” deadlines (project timeline, lines of effort), requiring follow-up to set concrete dates
  • Volunteer recruitment plan still unresolved, dependent on sponsor needs not yet finalized
  • Storage logistics for collected bedding not yet determined
  • Communication structure (single point of contact) identified as needed but not yet established

What We Learned

  • Reverse planning from the April 25 build date is essential for identifying critical path activities
  • Project scope should focus on four “lines of effort”: manpower, materials, location, and logistics
  • Not all SHP builds are public; understanding the difference between closed and open builds is important for volunteer coordination
  • Bedding drive requires targeting 40 twin “bed-in-a-bag” sets, sourced from retailers like Amazon, Walmart, and Kohl’s
  • A single point of contact for communication with Paul is critical for clear, efficient coordination

PM Takeaway

Reverse Planning & Lines of Effort: Paul’s emphasis on working backwards from the April 25 build date provided a clear framework for milestone planning. Organizing work into distinct “lines of effort” (manpower, materials, location, logistics) gives the team a structured way to divide responsibilities and track progress independently across each area.

Charter Finalization & Fundraising Strategy

February 24, 2026

What Went Well

  • Completed and sent founding documents (project charter, scope statement, communication management plan) to Paul Mele for review
  • Full team attendance with focused 30-minute meeting
  • Brainstormed multiple fundraising approaches: downtown Blacksburg outreach, personal networks, and corporate sponsorship

Challenges Encountered

  • No clear path yet for securing 40 beds-in-a-bag — fundraising vs. corporate sponsors still undecided
  • Corporate sponsor outreach had not yet started
  • Short meeting window (30 minutes) limited depth of discussion on fundraising tactics

What We Learned

  • Finalizing documents early frees the team to shift focus entirely to execution
  • Fundraising requires multiple channels — personal networks, local businesses, and online campaigns — rather than relying on a single source
  • A focused agenda keeps short meetings productive and prevents scope creep in discussions

PM Takeaway

Document Closure & Transition to Execution: Completing founding documents and submitting them for stakeholder review marks a clear transition from planning to execution. This milestone allowed the team to shift focus from documentation to fundraising and outreach with confidence that the project’s foundation was solid.

Progress Reporting & Execution Coordination

March 20, 2026

What Went Well

  • Strong team morale — the team felt ahead of other groups in the class with multiple deliverables already completed
  • Clear task division for Progress Report 2: each member assigned a distinct section for parallel writing
  • Six key project documents already completed (communication plan, charter, scope statement, schedule, communication list, impact matrix)
  • Proactive outreach plan established: in-person store visits, flyer distribution, and continued digital fundraising

Challenges Encountered

  • Still need 32 more beds-in-a-bag — significant gap remains in bedding collection
  • Paul had not yet responded with full delivery logistics information
  • Presentation content structure not yet finalized despite approaching deadline
  • Tight deadlines for multiple concurrent deliverables (report, presentation, continued outreach)

What We Learned

  • Dividing writing responsibilities by section enables parallel progress and faster turnaround on team deliverables
  • Maintaining a shared document platform (Google Drive) reduces coordination friction and keeps everyone aligned
  • Proactive in-person outreach (visiting local stores, distributing flyers) complements digital fundraising and expands community reach
  • Regular status checks help the team identify gaps early — knowing 32 beds are still needed creates urgency for targeted action

PM Takeaway

Parallel Workstreams & Proactive Outreach: Splitting the progress report among five members demonstrates effective work breakdown structure in action. Combining digital fundraising (GoFundMe, Amazon Wishlist) with in-person outreach (store visits, flyer distribution) creates multiple touchpoints for community support and reduces dependency on any single fundraising channel.

Final Push & Build Day Preparation

April 21, 2026

What Went Well

  • Two members (Holden, Andres) kept the meeting productive and decisive even with reduced attendance
  • Concrete plan finalized to close the bedding gap through a downtown tabling fundraiser (Gatorade/water sales to bar-goers)
  • Lines-of-effort planning (manpower, materials, logistics, timeline) anchored the upcoming sponsor coordination meeting with Paul Mele
  • Tight near-term deadlines set with explicit owners: tabling location by 4/22, build plan by 4/23, downtown tabling by 4/24

Challenges Encountered

  • Three of five team members absent — reduced bandwidth in the final week before the build
  • Still 12 beds-in-a-bag short of the 40-bed goal with only days remaining
  • Pressure to convert short-window fundraising into bedding purchases before 4/25

What We Learned

  • Late-stage gaps require tactical fundraising channels (event-based tabling) rather than slower digital campaigns
  • A small core team can still move execution forward when ownership of action items is unambiguous
  • Build-day success depends on lines-of-effort discipline negotiated directly with the sponsor, not just internal team planning
  • A pre-build sponsor meeting is the right forum to lock in venue contingency, materials timing, and volunteer assignments

PM Takeaway

Closing the Gap Under Time Pressure: The final week revealed that even a well-planned project needs an adaptive fundraising tactic to absorb residual gaps. Locking in a sponsor sync and four named action items days before the build kept critical path activities from slipping — a reminder that explicit owners and tight due dates are the most reliable risk mitigation in the closeout phase.
